**Chapter 1**   
**INTRODUCTION**

In an educational institution, the commitment of the faculty is critical to developing the institution (Naveed et al., 2019). Although the term “faculty” reflects a particular role of the traditional teaching staff, it has a considerably wider scope (Duaa AlSaeed, 2020).Modern faculty members are expected to perform diverse academic roles that require specialized knowledge, professional skills, and the ability to adapt to evolving educational demands. As higher education institutions continue to develop and diversify academic roles, faculty members must respond to changing teaching responsibilities and institutional expectations (Kenny & Fluck, 2022). Faculty members are responsible not only for delivering quality instruction but also for performing various academic and administrative responsibilities that contribute to the effective operation of an institution. According to Duaa AlSaeed 2020, with the changing times, the landscape of education has changed as well. Because of the globalization of education, one can find multiple streams in different domains, thereby signaling a strategic change from the traditional educational framework to a considerably modern framework. With the increasing number of responsibilities assigned to faculty members, proper workload planning and management become essential to ensure that tasks are distributed fairly and efficiently. Modern faculty members also face challenges in handling unpredictable changes and various issues related to their schedules. Sudden changes in class schedules, meetings, assigned tasks, deadlines, and other institutional activities may affect their ability to manage their workload effectively. These challenges can make it difficult for faculty members to balance teaching responsibilities, administrative duties, and other academic tasks while maintaining the quality of education they provide. 

Faculty workload remains an important concern in higher education institutions, as faculty members are expected to balance teaching, research, and service-related responsibilities. These responsibilities often compete for faculty members’ time, making effective workload distribution essential to maintaining a manageable balance among academic duties (Kenny & Fluck, 2022). Therefore, implementing an organized system for managing faculty workloads can help educational institutions monitor assignments, improve workload tracking, and support faculty members in fulfilling their responsibilities effectively.

Moreover, the issue prevalently seems to exist with the administrators who fail to allocate workload in an efficient and fair manner. In addition, there seems to be a lack of a mechanism for measuring the workload allocated to the faculty (Duaa Alsaaed, 2020). This situation shows the need for a faster and more efficient way of managing and allocating faculty workloads. A more organized process can help administrators save time, reduce errors, ensure fair distribution of tasks, and respond more quickly to changes in faculty schedules and responsibilities. Therefore, improving the current workload allocation process is important to help both administrators and faculty members manage their responsibilities more effectively. 

This study proposes the development of a more organized and efficient approach to managing faculty information and workload through FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System. At present, the lack of a proper system for monitoring and measuring faculty workloads can make it difficult for administrators to determine whether tasks are distributed fairly among faculty members. As a result, some faculty members may receive too many teaching loads and responsibilities, while others may have fewer assigned tasks. The manual process of recording faculty information, assigning workloads, and managing schedules may also take more time and can lead to errors, schedule conflicts, and inefficient workload distribution. 

This gap is evident in the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department. To address this concern, FACILoad is proposed as a system that can provide a more organized and efficient way of managing faculty information and workloads. The system can help administrators store and manage faculty information in one centralized platform, making records easier to access and update. It can also assist in recording, assigning, and monitoring teaching loads and other faculty responsibilities to promote a fairer distribution of workloads. In addition, FACILoad can help manage faculty schedules and identify possible schedule conflicts or overlapping assignments. The system can provide a faster way to allocate workloads, reduce errors caused by manual recording, and allow administrators to monitor the workload of each faculty member. It can also generate organized workload reports that may help the administrators and Heads of Departments make better decisions when assigning and balancing faculty responsibilities. 

As stipulated by Liu et al. (2022), a specifically designed faculty workload system not only improves work efficiency but also ensures that human resource wastage is reduced or minimized. Moreover, Altinay (2016) also pointed out that capitalizing on digital technology transforms school management for the better which in turn will translate to the quality of services that educational institutions can offer. This  is  in consideration to the fact that  faculty manual workload management has high error rate which can affect the quality and equity of load evaluation and distribution.This issue holds much significance especially in higher education institutions where faculty members perform a variety of tasks aside from instruction such as research and even community service (AlSaeed, 2020).  

To address these issues, this study delves into the proposed system that will provide a more efficient way of storing, updating, and retrieving faculty information while allowing administrators and department heads to monitor teaching loads and assignments more accurately. Through the integration of these functions into a single system, the study seeks to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and organization of faculty workload management within the institution. The system will incorporate features that allow authorized users to manage faculty profiles, record teaching assignments, monitor individual workloads, and generate organized reports for administrative purposes. By integrating these functions into a single platform, the system seeks to make faculty information and workload management more accessible, accurate, and efficient. It will also provide administrators with updated information that can support the proper distribution and monitoring of faculty workloads. Furthermore, the system aims to improve record organization and reduce the difficulties associated with scattered or manually maintained records.

The proposed system will be developed as a web-based information management platform, allowing authorized users to access and manage faculty records through a structured and user-friendly interface. Role-based access may be incorporated to ensure that faculty members, department heads, and administrators can perform functions according to their respective responsibilities. Through these features, the system seeks to improve the efficiency of faculty information management, support more organized workload monitoring, and provide a reliable digital record that can be used for academic and administrative purposes.

**Theoretical/ Conceptual Framework**

The study is supported by the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology 2 (UTAUT2) proposed by Venkatesh, Thong, and Xu (2012). UTAUT2 explains technology acceptance and use through factors such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, hedonic motivation, price value, and habit. In this study, performance expectancy relates to the extent to which users believe that the proposed faculty scheduling information system can improve scheduling processes, provide convenient access to faculty timetables, and support the management of scheduling information. Effort expectancy refers to how easily users can navigate and operate the system, while social influence concerns the impact of colleagues and administrators on their willingness to use it. Facilitating conditions relate to the availability of the necessary technological resources and support for effective system use. Furthermore, habit may influence how regularly faculty members access the system to check their schedules, while hedonic motivation may relate to their satisfaction when interacting with the system. These concepts are important because the effectiveness of the proposed system depends not only on its technical capabilities but also on users' acceptance, willingness to use it, and ability to integrate it into their academic and administrative activities.

The study is supported by the Human–Organization–Technology Fit (HOT-Fit) model developed by Yusof et al. (2006). The HOT-Fit model evaluates information systems by examining three main components: human, organization, and technology, including the relationships and alignment among these components. In this study, the human component relates to faculty members' and administrators' use of the proposed faculty scheduling information system, their satisfaction, and their perceptions of its usefulness and ease of use. The organizational component concerns the system's alignment with the institution's scheduling procedures, administrative requirements, and available support. Meanwhile, the technology component focuses on the system's quality, including the accuracy, completeness, availability, security, and timeliness of scheduling information. These components are important because the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed system depend not only on its technical performance but also on its suitability for users' needs and the institution's operational processes. By applying the HOT-Fit model, the study can assess how well the system supports faculty scheduling activities, improves access to scheduling information, and contributes to more effective and efficient scheduling management.

With both theories, the study explains how the proposed Faculty Information and Load Management System can be evaluated in terms of user acceptance, system usability, effectiveness, efficiency, and suitability for its intended users and organizational environment. The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology 2 (UTAUT2) provides a foundation for understanding technology acceptance by examining factors such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, hedonic motivation, price value, and habit. These factors help explain users' willingness to adopt and use the proposed system in managing faculty schedules and accessing scheduling information. Meanwhile, the Human–Organization–Technology Fit (HOT-Fit) model complements this framework by examining the alignment among human factors, organizational requirements, and technological capabilities to evaluate the system's effectiveness and efficiency. Together, these frameworks support the assessment of whether the proposed system meets users' needs, provides accurate and accessible scheduling information, supports faculty workload management, and aligns with the institution's scheduling processes and requirements.

**Purpose and Description**

The purpose of this study is to develop **FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System**, a web-based system designed to provide a more organized, efficient, and systematic approach to managing faculty information and workloads in the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department. The proposed system aims to address the challenges encountered in the manual recording, assignment, monitoring, and management of faculty workloads, teaching assignments, and schedules. By providing a centralized system, FACILoad seeks to reduce the time and effort required in handling faculty workload-related tasks while improving the organization and accessibility of important records.

FACILoad will provide a centralized platform where authorized users can record, update, manage, and retrieve faculty information, teaching assignments, workloads, and schedules. The system will allow users to maintain organized faculty records and monitor the teaching loads assigned to each faculty member. It will also assist in managing faculty schedules and identifying possible schedule conflicts or overlapping assignments. Through these functions, the system can help reduce errors that may occur during manual recording and workload preparation and provide a more systematic method of managing faculty assignments.

The system will also include workload monitoring and report generation features that can provide administrators and department heads with organized information regarding faculty assignments and workload distribution. These reports may serve as references when reviewing faculty workloads and managing teaching responsibilities. By having faculty information and workload records stored in one centralized platform, authorized users can more easily access and update information without relying heavily on scattered or manually maintained records.

Furthermore, FACILoad is intended to support administrators and department heads in monitoring and managing faculty workloads more efficiently. The system will provide a structured and user-friendly interface where users can perform workload-related tasks according to their respective responsibilities. It will also help maintain updated digital records that can be accessed when needed for administrative and academic purposes. The system is designed to minimize repetitive paperwork, improve record organization, and provide more accessible workload information.

Overall, FACILoad aims to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and organization of faculty information and workload management within the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department. Through the integration of faculty records, teaching assignments, workload monitoring, schedule management, conflict identification, and report generation into a single platform, the proposed system seeks to provide a more systematic approach to managing faculty workloads and supporting the administrative processes of the department.

### **Scope and Delimitation**

This study focuses on the development of FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System for the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department. The proposed system is designed to provide a centralized and organized platform for managing faculty information, teaching assignments, workloads, and schedules. It will cover the recording, updating, storing, and retrieval of faculty information, as well as the assignment and monitoring of teaching workloads. The system will allow authorized users to manage faculty records and view the workload distribution of individual faculty members.

The system will also include features for managing faculty schedules and identifying possible schedule conflicts or overlapping assignments. It will provide workload computation to assist in determining the assigned workload of faculty members and will generate organized workload reports that administrators and department heads can use for monitoring and administrative purposes. By integrating these functions into one platform, the system aims to reduce the time and effort required for manual workload preparation, improve record organization, and provide easier access to faculty workload information. These functions are consistent with the proposed system's focus on centralized faculty records, workload monitoring, schedule management, conflict identification, and report generation.

The study is limited to faculty information and workload management within the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department. It will not cover other institutional processes that are not directly related to faculty workload management, such as student enrollment, student grading, payroll and salary processing, attendance monitoring, financial management, or other general administrative functions. The system will also not independently make final decisions regarding faculty workload distribution, as workload assignments and related administrative decisions will remain under the responsibility of authorized department personnel.

      Furthermore, the system will depend on the accuracy and completeness of the information entered by authorized users. The study will focus on the workload management needs and processes identified within the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department and may not directly represent the requirements of other departments or institutions. Future expansion to other departments, integration with other institutional systems, or additional administrative functions will be outside the scope of the present study.

**REVIEW RELATED LITERATURE** 

       This chapter presents a review of literature and studies from books, peer-reviewed journals, and academic databases relevant to the development of FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System. It provides theoretical and empirical perspectives on faculty information management, assignment distribution, scheduling, monitoring, and the use of information systems in educational institutions. It also examines existing systems and previous studies concerning the challenges of manually managing faculty records, assignments, schedules, monitoring, and documentation. These sources provide a foundation for identifying existing issues and determining suitable features and approaches for the proposed system. The review is divided into **f**oreign and local literature and studies, providing a broader perspective on faculty management practices and information systems in different educational settings. The findings from these sources will help identify the research gap and establish the relevance of FACILoad to the administrative needs of the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department.

**Foreign Literature**

Chantrapornchai (2012) conducted a study entitled “Development of a Faculty Work Load System: A Case Study in a Public University” at Silpakorn University in Thailand. The study focused on developing an information system that can help organize and manage the workload of faculty members. It considered different responsibilities such as teaching, research, and administrative work. The study gathered information about the tasks performed by faculty members and proposed an integrated system that could show the workload of each faculty member and department. The developed system was intended to make workload management more organized and efficient. This study is relevant to the present research because it shows how an information system can be used to properly monitor, organize, and manage workloads in an educational institution.

Fitzpatrick, Millette-Snodgrass, and Atef (2016) conducted a study entitled “A Novel Mathematical Model for Determining Faculty Workload”at California Northstate University College of Pharmacy, USA. The study developed a mathematical model to determine faculty workload by measuring activities such as teaching, service, scholarship, professional development, and clinical practice based on time spent and activity scores. The findings showed that faculty members had different workload levels depending on their responsibilities. The model helped the institution understand how faculty time was distributed and manage workloads more effectively. The researchers concluded that workload analysis is useful for managing faculty responsibilities and that workload models should be regularly updated based on changes in faculty activities and institutional needs.

Akampurira and Kazaara (2024) conducted a study entitled “Exploring the Relationship Between Faculty Workload and Student Success: A Case Study of Metropolitan International University” at Metropolitan International University in Kampala, Uganda. The study examined the relationship between faculty workload and student success. The findings showed that higher faculty workload was associated with lower student academic performance and retention rates. It also found that faculty job satisfaction had a positive relationship with student engagement and academic achievement. The researchers emphasized the importance of effectively managing faculty workload to promote faculty well-being and improve student outcomes.

**Local Literature**

In educational institutions across the Philippines, including Palompon Institute of Technology, organizing faculty records and managing teaching assignments is one of the most essential yet difficult daily tasks. Many departments still rely mostly on manual methods: using paper forms, logbooks, and separate spreadsheets to keep personal details, track teaching units, prepare schedules, and distribute workloads. According to Austero, Sison, & Matias (November 2022), manual preparation of class schedules and faculty loads every semester is laborious, time-consuming, and prone to errors, conflicts and overlaps are often discovered only after schedules are released. Similarly, Ortega, Dela Torre, & Brigoli (2026) stated that manual scheduling takes weeks of trial-and-error, produces inconsistent records, and requires repeated corrections that delay final schedules. 

Without a proper and centralized system, faculty information and teaching loads are kept in different places making it hard to verify complete details, check the distribution of work, and ensure fairness in assigning teaching hours. Maribao (May 2026\) explained that separate records and manual computation lead to unequal workload distribution some instructors receive too many units while others have fewer than required—because there is no single shared database to check assignments against. Ortega et al. (2026) also noted that when data is scattered across spreadsheets and logbooks, adjustments cannot be announced quickly; faculty members remain uncertain about their actual load and schedule until the final paper is released . 

Developing a Faculty Information and Load Management System made specifically for the Industrial Technology Department serves as a direct and practical solution. Storing all necessary data in one centralized system faculty profiles, teaching assignments, units, and schedules makes records consistent, easy to retrieve, and simple to update. As proven by Ortega et al. (2026), a computerized framework reduces scheduling time significantly and produces more acceptable, conflict-free faculty loading assignments . Maribao (2026) further confirmed that an automated system enables authorized personnel to assign and adjust workloads accurately in minutes instead of days, while faculty members can view their own schedules and load details anytime. 

Similarly, literature also emphasizes that building a system focused only on what the department truly needs makes it more useful and easier to maintain. Stephano et al. (2026) stated that systems designed with clear, limited scope rather than including unnecessary features are simpler to operate, easier to maintain, and more readily adopted by users. Restricting access to authorized personnel only, as noted in the Decision Support System on Faculty Profiling (IIETA, March 2025), protects sensitive faculty information, prevents unauthorized changes, and keeps the system manageable and secure for department-level use. 

Overall, local literature strongly supports the development of this system. Replacing manual recording with a proper centralized system improves accuracy, saves working hours, helps ensure fair distribution of teaching loads, and makes information clear and accessible. Austero et al. (2022), Ortega et al. (2026), and Maribao (2026) all agree: computerized management resolves common manual problems conflicts, delays, unfair distribution, and lost records resulting in smoother department operations and better organization for both administration and faculty.

**Local Studies**

       Faculty workload management has been a recurring challenge in Philippine higher education institutions. Perez (2025) studied contract-of-service faculty at Pampanga State University and found that excessive workloads hindered timely feedback and course innovation, while multitasking negatively affected teaching effectiveness. Similarly, Ancho and Bongco (2019) highlighted how Filipino teachers’ increasing professional workload compromises instructional quality and faculty well-being, stressing the need for institutional workload policies that balance teaching and administrative duties.  

      Local system development efforts also attempted to automate workload processes. Medallo et al. (2026) designed an integrated faculty loading, room, and class scheduling system for Madridejos Community College, which significantly reduced scheduling conflicts and improved workload equity. Diegas and Dumaguit (2026) developed an integrated academic operations and faculty management system at Agusan del Sur State College, which streamlined workload management, reporting, and performance monitoring through centralized data management and AI-based features.  

    Optimization approaches have also been explored. Espenilla et al. (2026) applied a priority-weighted multi-objective Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) model at the University of the Philippines Diliman to assign faculty courses based on preferences, eligibility, and institutional policies. (2025) conducted a systematic review on predictive analytics in faculty performance evaluation, showing how workload balance and ICT integration are critical determinants in forecasting faculty effectiveness.

However, gaps persist in fully integrated systems that combine automated computation, workload monitoring, report generation, and centralized information management. **Synthesis**

       The reviewed foreign and local literature and studies show that effective faculty management is important in maintaining organized academic operations and supporting both faculty performance and student outcomes. Foreign studies demonstrate that information systems and workload models can help institutions monitor faculty responsibilities, understand workload distribution, and improve the management of teaching and administrative tasks. Local literature and studies similarly reveal that manual processes often result in time-consuming work, scheduling conflicts, inaccurate records, and unequal distribution of teaching assignments. Several local studies have shown that computerized and centralized systems can improve scheduling, reporting, monitoring, and the accuracy of faculty assignments. However, the reviewed studies also indicate a gap in systems that integrate faculty information, workload computation, monitoring, scheduling, and report generation in one platform specifically suited to a department's needs. In response to this gap, FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System is proposed to provide a centralized and user-friendly platform for managing faculty information and teaching assignments in the Industrial Technology Department of Palompon Institute of Technology.

**DESIGN AND METHODOLOGY**

**Research Design**

This study will employ a developmental research design, since it is primarily directed toward the design, development, and evaluation of a functional information system, together with a descriptive-evaluative method used to assess the performance and quality of the developed system based on the feedback of its intended users. Developmental research is described by Richey and Klein (2007) as the systematic study of designing, developing, and evaluating instructional and non-instructional products, tools, and new or enhanced models, together with the processes that govern their development. This design is deemed appropriate for the present study since FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System will be designed, developed, and refined through the Iterative and Incremental Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC), as discussed in the conceptual framework of the study.

In operationalizing this design, the study will proceed through the following stages: (1) requirements gathering and analysis, wherein the researchers will identify the existing faculty workload management practices, problems, and needs of the Industrial Technology Department through interviews and observation; (2) system design, wherein the data design, system architecture, and user interface of FACILoad will be planned based on the identified requirements; (3) development, wherein the actual features of the system, such as faculty information management, workload assignment, workload monitoring, scheduling, and report generation, will be programmed and integrated; (4) testing and evaluation, wherein the developed system will be checked for functionality and will be evaluated by the identified respondents; (5) user feedback and refinement, wherein issues identified during testing will be addressed; and (6) deployment, wherein the finalized system will be turned over for use by the Industrial Technology Department. The descriptive-evaluative component of the design will be applied during the testing and evaluation stage, in which a survey questionnaire rated on a five-point Likert scale will be used to gather the respondents' assessment of the system's functionality, usability, user experience, learnability, and accessibility, consistent with the Method of Scoring discussed in this chapter.

**Research Environment**

This study will be conducted at Palompon Institute of Technology, a state university located in Poblacion, Palompon, Leyte, Philippines. Specifically, the study will focus on the Industrial Technology Department under the College of Technology and Engineering, which is the intended end-user and beneficiary of the proposed FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System.

The Industrial Technology Department was chosen as the locale of the study because it is the department directly experiencing the difficulties associated with the manual preparation, recording, monitoring, and management of faculty workloads, as identified in the background of the study. The department office, faculty room, and computer laboratories of the Industrial Technology Department will serve as the primary venues for conducting interviews and observations during the requirements-gathering stage, as well as for the deployment and evaluation of the developed system.

**Population of the Study**

The respondents of this study will be composed of the faculty members, Department Head, and authorized administrative personnel of the Industrial Technology Department of Palompon Institute of Technology, who are considered the primary and intended users of the proposed FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System. These respondents were selected because they are the ones directly involved in the preparation, assignment, monitoring, and management of faculty workloads within the department, making them the most qualified to evaluate the relevance, functionality, and usability of the developed system.

Since the Industrial Technology Department has a limited number of faculty members and administrative personnel, the study will make use of total enumeration or complete enumeration technique, wherein all qualified members of the population, consisting of 25 faculty members and 5 department personnel (Department Head and/or Program Coordinator), for a total of 30 respondents, will be included as evaluators of the system. This sampling technique was deemed appropriate given the small and clearly defined population, ensuring that the results of the evaluation reflect the collective assessment of the department rather than that of a limited sample.

**Research Instrument**

      The research instrument used in this study was a structured system evaluation questionnaire developed to assess the FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System based on the objectives, system requirements, and intended functions identified in the study. The questionnaire was designed to gather quantitative data from the intended users regarding the quality and effectiveness of the developed system in managing faculty information, teaching workloads, scheduling, workload computation, conflict detection, and workload reporting.

     The questionnaire consisted of six parts. The first part evaluated Functional Suitability, focusing on the system's ability to perform the required functions for faculty information management, workload assignment and monitoring, workload computation, schedule management, conflict detection, and report generation. The second part evaluated Performance Efficiency, particularly the system's response, processing time, and ability to reduce the time and effort required in workload and scheduling activities. The third part assessed Usability and Operability, including ease of navigation, clarity of the interface, error prevention, and ease of performing workload-related tasks. The fourth part evaluated Reliability, particularly the system's availability, consistency, data preservation, and ability to provide accurate workload and schedule information. The fifth part assessed Security and Accountability, including authorized access, role-based access, protection of faculty information, and the recording and management of system activities. The sixth part provided an Overall Assessment of the system's usefulness, effectiveness, organization, accessibility, and contribution to the management of faculty workloads and schedules.

   A five-point Likert scale was used to measure the respondents' level of agreement with each statement, ranging from 5 (Strongly Agree) to 1 (Strongly Disagree). The questionnaire was administered to the identified users of the system, particularly the Department Chair/Department Head, faculty members, and authorized personnel, whose responses served as the basis for evaluating the developed system and identifying areas for further improvement and refinement.

**Requirements and Specifications**

**Operational Feasibility**

The FACILoad system is designed to support the existing operations of the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department at Palompon Institute of Technology. It provides an organized and efficient approach to managing faculty schedules and computing teaching workloads. Administrators can manage and update faculty information, workload assignments, class schedules, and records of sections, rooms, and subjects. Meanwhile, faculty members can view their assigned workloads and class schedules, specify their academic functions and designations, and add their additional teaching sessions. Through its user-friendly interface and basic user orientation, the system aims to reduce manual tasks, minimize errors, and improve the overall efficiency of faculty workload and scheduling management.

**Functional Requirements**

**1\. Login and User Authentication**: The system should allow authorized users to log in using their registered accounts to access the system.

**2\. Faculty Information Managemen**t: The system should allow administrators to add, view, update, and delete faculty information.

**3\. Faculty Workload Management**: The system should allow authorized users to record, assign, and manage teaching loads and other faculty responsibilities.

**4\. Workload Monitoring**: The system should allow the Department Head and administrators to monitor and view the workload distribution of each faculty member.

**5\. Schedule Management**: The system should allow authorized users to create, view, update, and delete faculty schedules, including assigned subjects, sections, rooms, and time schedules.

**6\. Schedule Conflict Detection**: The system should automatically detect overlapping schedules and conflicts involving faculty members, subjects, sections, rooms, and assigned time.

**7\. Workload Computatio**n: The system should automatically calculate the total working hours and assigned workload of each faculty member based on their recorded teaching assignments.

**8\. Faculty Schedule Viewing**: The system should allow faculty members to view their assigned teaching schedules and workload information.

**9\. Workload Report Generation**: The system should generate organized faculty workload reports that administrators and the Department Head can use for monitoring and reviewing faculty assignments.

**10\. Room Managemen**t: The system should allow authorized users to add, view, update, and delete room records used for scheduling.

**11\. Subject Management**: The system should allow authorized users to add, view, update, and delete subject records needed for faculty workload assignments.

**12\. Section Managemen**t: The system should allow authorized users to add, view, update, and delete section records used in faculty scheduling.

**13\. Logout**: The system should allow users to securely log out of their accounts after using the system.

**Non-Functional Requirements**

**1\. Usability**: The system should be easy to understand and use, especially for faculty and administrators.

**2\. Performance**: The system should respond quickly when users access, update, or retrieve information.

**3\. Reliability**: The system should provide accurate and consistent faculty workload and scheduling information.

**4\. Security**: The system should protect faculty information from unauthorized access or changes.

**5\. Scalability**: The system should allow additional faculty records and information to be added as needed.

**6\. Maintainability**: The system should be easy to update, troubleshoot, and maintain when improvements are needed.

**7\. Data Integrity**: The system should maintain complete and accurate records to prevent errors in faculty workload and scheduling.

**Data Design**

**![][image5]**

**Figure 5\. Entity Relationship Diagram**

Figure 5, presents the Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) for the FACILoad System. It consists of 12 main entities which are users, faculty, faculty designations, workload assignments, subjects, sections, rooms, schedule slots, designation sessions, other function sessions, and class program student counts. The **Users** entity stores account information, including User ID (primary key), email, password hash, role, and faculty ID (foreign key), which links user accounts to the **Faculty** entity. The Faculty entity contains faculty information such as employee number, first name, last name, academic rank, employment status, and maximum units. The **Workload Assignments** entity is connected to the Faculty, Subjects, Sections, and Academic Terms entities through their respective foreign keys, recording the subjects assigned to faculty members for specific sections and academic terms. Similarly, the **Schedule Slots** entity references Workload Assignments and Rooms to store scheduling details, including section type, day of the week, start time, and end time. The **Academic Terms** entity stores the school year, semester, and active status, while the Sections and Rooms entities maintain information about class sections and available facilities. Furthermore, the **Faculty Designations** entity records the designations assigned to faculty members for a particular academic term, while the **Designation Sessions** entity stores their corresponding designation schedules and session details. The **Other Function Sessions** entity records additional faculty functions, including their function type, title, and assigned schedule. Lastly, the **Class Program Student Counts** entity tracks the number of students in specific sections for each faculty member and academic term. These relationships establish connections between faculty information, teaching workloads, class schedules, academic designations, and additional faculty responsibilities, providing an organized database structure for managing and monitoring faculty workloads and schedules within the FACILoad system.

### **Figure 6\. Use Case Diagram**

Figure 6 shows the object-oriented models of the FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System, illustrating how the Faculty and Chairman users interact with the different functions of the system. For both users, the process begins with Login, which provides access to their respective dashboards, the Faculty Dashboard and the Chairman Dashboard. The Chairman logs in through Login and Registration.

From the Faculty Dashboard, the faculty member can access the Account Management, Personal Schedule, Subjects, Faculty Profile, Other Assignments, Action Taken, Class Program, Room, and Section modules. Through these modules, the faculty member can mainly view records related to their own teaching load. Account Management allows them to view their profile and change their password. Personal Schedule lets them view their own class schedule and check the time range of their assigned classes. The Subjects module shows their assigned subjects, including subject details and lecture and lab hours, while Faculty Profile presents their employment details, specialization, and attained degree. Other Assignments shows the duration hours of any additional assignments. Through Action Taken, the faculty member can view the action status and sign the document as a signer. The Class Program, Room, and Section modules provide access to the student class program, room information, and section information.

From the Chairman Dashboard, the chairman can access the Account Management, Schedule Management, Faculty Management, Room Management, Subject Management, Section Management, Other Assignment Management, Action Approval, and Class Program Management modules. Through these modules, the chairman can view, add, edit, and delete the corresponding records. Account Management allows the chairman to view their profile and change their password. Schedule Management handles faculty schedules and includes checks for hour limits and schedule conflicts, as well as viewing of remaining hours. Faculty Management handles faculty records, including toggling faculty status and viewing faculty information. Room Management organizes room records and supports toggling room availability, viewing room schedule data, updating the room signature, and viewing room utilization. Subject Management maintains subject records and allows updates to subject students and total students, while Section Management maintains section records. Other Assignment Management handles additional faculty assignments and includes checks for assignment conflicts and edit permissions.

The Action Approval module allows the chairman to sign documents as prepared, reviewed, verified, and approved, and to review overtime and service credits. Class Program Management lets the chairman view the student class program and update its term, effective date, remarks, and action taken. Both users are also provided with a Logout function to securely end their sessions. Overall, the diagrams illustrate the flow of user interaction, with the Faculty user focused on viewing and signing functions, and the Chairman user focused on the main management and approval functions of FACILoad.

**Implementation Plan**

The implementation of FACILoad: Faculty Information and Load Management System will be carried out through a series of organized steps to ensure that the system functions properly and meets the requirements of the Bachelor of Industrial Technology Department. The implementation will focus on deploying the developed system, testing its functions, and making necessary improvements before its actual use.

Implementation Steps:

1. Prepare the Final System – Finalize the developed FACILoad system, including faculty information management, workload assignment, workload monitoring, scheduling, workload computation, conflict detection, and report generation.  
2. Set Up the System – Install and configure the required software, database, and system files on the designated computers or server that will be used by the Industrial Technology Department.  
3. Input Initial Faculty Data – Enter the necessary faculty information, teaching assignments, schedules, and workload records into the centralized system to prepare it for actual use.  
4. Conduct System Testing – Test all major functions of FACILoad to verify its functionality, accuracy, reliability, performance, and ability to detect scheduling conflicts and calculate workloads correctly.  
5. User Evaluation and Feedback – Allow the Department Head, faculty members, and authorized personnel to use and evaluate the system based on its functionality, usability, user experience, learnability, and accessibility.  
6. Refinement and Correction – Review the feedback and test results, identify errors or areas that need improvement, and make the necessary adjustments to the system.  
7. Deployment and Turnover – Deploy the finalized FACILoad system for use by the Industrial Technology Department and provide the authorized users with the necessary instructions for accessing and using the system.  
8. Monitoring and Maintenance – Monitor the system after deployment and perform necessary updates, troubleshooting, and maintenance to ensure that faculty workload and scheduling information remain accurate and reliable.
